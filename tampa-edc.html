INTERVIEW CHALLENGE · ECONOMIC ANALYSIS PROJECT

Distribution
& Logistics

An interview project exploring how to turn regional employment data into an evidence-based location argument, from Excel analysis to a final investor-focused presentation.

EXCEL2015–2025 INDUSTRY COMPARISONLOCATION QUOTIENTSECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Why should Investors locate in Tampa or Hillsborough?

As a part of an interview process, I was given a small data set on Distribution & Logistics Industry, Occupational, and Establishment counts across Hillsborough County, Tampa MSA, and Florida from 2015-2025. I was prompted with coming up with my own analysis using any combination of this data as to why an investor may choose to locate in Tampa Bay or Hillsborough. I was also prompted to use any data visualization tool to support my analysis.

02 / UNDERSTANDING THE DATA

Turning an open brief into questions I could investigate.

I began by considering what each part of the dataset could tell an investor. Industry employment described the business sectors present in each region, occupation counts offered a view of the workforce, and establishment counts added context about the businesses supporting that employment. These gave me several ways to explore the location question before choosing a focus for the presentation.

03 / EXPLORING DIFFERENT MEASURES

Choosing comparisons that answered different questions.

My Excel workbook includes several exploratory views. Each offered a different perspective on the region and helped me think through which measures would be most useful for the final story.

Jobs per establishment

I brought employment and establishment counts together to compare average jobs per business. This gave me a way to explore business scale alongside the overall size of an industry.

Occupation comparisons

I organized occupation employment by region and year and used a pivot table and chart to explore the workforce mix. This helped me consider the types of work represented in the dataset alongside its industry categories.

Industry location quotients

I compared each industry’s share of regional employment with its share of national employment. This let me examine specialization while accounting for the different sizes of the regional economies.

Occupation location quotients

I applied the same share-based comparison to occupations. Keeping the industry and occupation views separate helped me distinguish the businesses located in a region from the kinds of work people perform.

04 / DEVELOPING THE GROWTH COMPARISON

Using a common starting point to compare change.

For the presentation, I focused on how industry employment changed from the same starting year. I organized the industry data by description, geography, employment, and year, then used an Excel pivot table and line chart to compare percentage change from the baseline. This made it possible to compare the regions’ growth trajectories despite their different employment totals. Keeping the baseline fixed also matched my question about growth over several years.

05 / BUILDING THE PRESENTATION

Moving from exploration to an argument an investor could follow.

Start with the overall comparison

I opened with the cluster-wide growth trend to establish the regional context before looking at individual industries.

Investigate the industry behind the story

I gave warehousing and storage its own slide so the audience could examine its trajectory separately from the cluster total.

Include the exceptions

I retained the consulting-services comparison where the local growth story was weaker. Showing that exception kept the argument tied to the evidence rather than implying that every industry followed the same pattern.

Condense the remaining comparisons

I used a table for the other industries, then closed with a location recommendation. This kept the presentation focused while giving the audience a way to compare the remaining results.

07 / WHAT THE PROJECT DEMONSTRATES

Making analytical choices and explaining them.

This interview project gave me a way to demonstrate how I work with an open question: explore the available measures, choose comparisons suited to the question, and organize the evidence into a presentation. The workbook captures the broader exploration, while the final deck focuses on the growth comparison and includes the exceptions. The project connects my economics background with Excel analysis and communicating a location argument to an audience.
