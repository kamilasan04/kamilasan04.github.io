01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Why should Investors locate in Tampa or Hillsborough?

As a part of an interview process, I was given a small data set on Distribution & Logistics Industry, Occupational, and Establishment counts across Hillsborough County, Tampa MSA, and Florida from 2015-2025. I was prompted with coming up with my own analysis using any combination of this data as to why an investor may choose to locate in Tampa Bay or Hillsborough. I was also prompted to use any data visualization tool to support my analysis.

02 / REGIONAL GROWTH

Hillsborough’s cluster employment nearly doubled.

Between 2015 and 2025, the county added 17,039 jobs in the dataset’s selected industry cluster. Its 90.4% growth exceeded Florida, Tampa MSA, and the national benchmark.

03 / LOCATION QUOTIENTS

Specialization varies within the cluster.

An LQ above 1 means an industry accounts for a larger share of local employment than it does nationally. Hillsborough’s overall cluster LQ of 1.16 indicates an employment share about 16% above the national share.

LQ = (local industry jobs ÷ local all-industry jobs) ÷ (U.S. industry jobs ÷ U.S. all-industry jobs)

04 / HILLSBOROUGH FINDINGS

A strong decade, with a more uneven recent picture.

Warehousing drove much of the expansion.

Warehousing and storage increased from 1,662 to 12,124 jobs: 10,462 additional jobs, or about 61% of the cluster’s net job gain. Its 2025 LQ of 1.26 also points to above-national concentration.

Growth did not extend equally to every industry.

General freight trucking increased from 3,722 to 5,406 jobs over the decade, but declined from 6,438 in 2022. Long-term growth can coexist with recent contraction and an LQ below 1.

The latest year moderated the growth story.

The county’s cluster total fell from 35,992 jobs in 2024 to 35,883 in 2025, a 0.3% decline. The decade-long comparison should not be read as uninterrupted expansion.

Workforce concentration adds another perspective.

The occupation analysis shows a 2025 Hillsborough LQ of 1.24 for business operations specialists and 0.96 for material moving workers. Occupation groups describe types of work across industries; they are not additional industry jobs to add to the cluster total.

05 / IMPLICATION

Use growth and concentration together.

The analysis identifies warehousing and freight transportation arrangement as areas for closer economic-development research, while showing why the broader cluster should not be treated as uniformly strong. Employment trends and LQs support descriptive comparisons; further evidence on wages, infrastructure, employer needs, and data definitions would be needed for a business-location recommendation.
